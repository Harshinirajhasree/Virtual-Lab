    Theory (from lab manual) & Quiz

    Key theory

    Ventricular fibrillation (asynchronous contraction of ventricular muscle) causes loss of cardiac output; irreversible brain damage or death may follow if not reversed within about 5 minutes. A high-energy DC shock makes all fibres contract together so the intrinsic pacemaker can resume. External defibrillation needs higher energy than internal (open-chest). A variable auto-transformer → high-voltage transformer → diode rectifier → changeover switch charges a capacitor (position 1); on shock the switch moves to position 2 and discharges through an inductor and electrodes. The inductor shapes the pulse but dissipates some energy. Patient resistance ≈ 50–100 Ω (≈80 cm² electrodes). Monophasic width 3–12 ms; biphasic positive 3–10 ms, negative 1–10 ms; biphasic gives higher efficacy. Synch mode delivers the shock on the R-wave (QRS) so it avoids the vulnerable T-wave.

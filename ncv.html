    Principle. A supramaximal stimulus at two points along the nerve produces a CMAP at the muscle. The latency is the time from stimulus artifact to CMAP onset. Distal latency includes neuromuscular junction and terminal delay; subtracting it from the proximal latency isolates conduction over the segment between the sites.

    CV (m/s) = Distance (mm) ÷ [Proximal latency (ms) − Distal latency (ms)] · Normal median motor NCV ≈ 50–70 m/s.

    Electrodes. Ra on the APB belly, Rr on the APB tendon (distal), GND on the dorsum of the hand. Sites. S1 wrist (between palmaris longus and FCR tendons, ~1 cm proximal to the crease), S2 elbow crease (medial to biceps tendon and brachial artery), S3 axilla.

    • Use supramaximal stimulation at every site.
    • Keep the limb warm – a cold limb falsely slows conduction (try the temperature slider).
    • Fix electrodes firmly; swapped or misplaced electrodes change the waveform here.
    • Keep the limb still to avoid artifacts.
    • Measure distance along the surface path of the nerve.

    All waveforms and values in this lab are generated by a simplified mathematical model with randomised subject parameters. They are for teaching only.
